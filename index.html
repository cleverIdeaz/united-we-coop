The scalable mechanic

Contribution earns attention. Attention can become support.

01

A co-op contributes

A treasurer, teacher, webmaster, board member, fundraiser, or parent volunteer hosts a practical session.

02

The network distributes

People discover the session by role and problem—not because they already know that school.

03

The school is featured

The event page pairs useful content with one transparent school need and a direct donation link.

04

The value stays alive

The recording, templates, and discussion become a searchable resource that keeps pointing back to its source.

THE FAIRNESS RULE

Schools do not need the biggest social following to benefit. The network rotates featured schools and rewards useful contribution, so attention can move toward the people doing the work.

Built to connect, not replace

The network can sit between the organizations and tools co-ops already trust.

V1 is intentionally light: no member accounts, no central wallet, no replacement school-management system. Verified school pages are published manually; events can use the host's existing video and registration tools; donations route directly to the recipient school.

What scale looks like

Thousands of co-ops do not mean thousands of competing fundraisers.

Live layer

A rotating calendar of specific, role-based conversations. The scarce thing is interaction—not information.

Library layer

Every session becomes durable public knowledge. Search once, learn from many schools.

Funding layer

Each session spotlights one or more verified schools. Donors choose where their support goes.

Sponsor layer

Later, values-aligned sponsors can underwrite a role track or match a round of school needs without owning the network.

The product is not the webinar. The product is continuity across co-ops.
The obvious questions

Keep the first version honest.

Who handles the money?

The school receiving the donation. United We Co-op links to each school's existing processor. V1 never pools or redistributes charitable funds.

Do people have to donate to learn?

No. Live participation can be capacity-limited, but knowledge stays accessible. Giving is a direct invitation: if the resource helped you, help the school that produced it.

Why repeat live sessions?

Because people, rules, tools, and problems change. Live rooms create new questions and relationships; the library captures the durable parts. Repetition is how the network stays current.

What happens when there are hundreds of schools?

Discovery becomes role- and problem-based. Schools take turns in featured rounds, co-host sessions, and contribute resources. The network can later add matching sponsors and regional or topic-based cohorts.

Does this replace Jovial or our school website?

No. Not in V1. It is a shared discovery, learning, and fundraising layer. Administrative software can remain exactly where it is until users prove a reason to change it.
